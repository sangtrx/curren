## What unique insights do you have about your users?

The expensive part of a trading-intelligence workflow is often not generating the first call; it is reconstructing the decision after it leaves the model. A human can infer from scattered messages that an entry changed, a target was hit, or a setup was invalidated. A software agent cannot safely make those inferences without explicit state and provenance.

That changes the product boundary. Once automated consumers are involved, timestamps, the original plan, append-only lifecycle changes, terminal outcomes, and permissions stop being audit garnish and become part of the intelligence itself. The same applies to evaluation: a historical result is weak evidence if you cannot show what was actually known before the move. Curren is built around that decision record rather than another mutable signal feed.

Truth note: this is a product/domain insight from building the workflow, not a claim of validated insight from a meaningful paying-user base. Curren remains pre-revenue and still needs willingness-to-pay validation.

## Why did you choose to work on this idea?

I started building the current Curren product in August 2026 after working on crypto signal, trading, and research systems. The recurring problem was not producing another market opinion. It was answering a much harder question later: **what did the system actually know and recommend at that moment, before the market moved?**

That problem showed up across research and live workflows. Backtests can look convincing if point-in-time boundaries are loose; live calls become hard to audit when entries, updates, invalidations, and outcomes are scattered across messages or mutable records. I chose Curren because I had already run into both sides of that trust problem myself and could build the full path from research evidence to live lifecycle to a sanitized machine-readable record.

## Describe an instance where you demonstrated exceptional creativity or perseverance

The clearest example is a research result I chose **not** to optimize away. I froze a representative campaign with point-in-time data, explicit out-of-sample/rejection gates, and no retuning. All **88/88** planned trials completed and **zero** representative candidates survived.

The easy response would have been to loosen the gates, rerun variants, or present only the best-looking backtest. Instead I kept the failure as durable evidence and rebuilt the workflow so rejected candidates remain part of the research record. That result also changed the product thesis: Curren should not ask users to trust a polished winning chart; it should make the original decision, its lifecycle, and its outcome verifiable from the start.
